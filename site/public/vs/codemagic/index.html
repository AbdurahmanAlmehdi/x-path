Comparison

XPath vs Codemagic

Codemagic is a hosted CI/CD service for mobile apps: your builds run on its Mac minis and Linux machines, and you pay by the build minute or a fixed yearly price. XPath is open source software you run on your own Mac or Linux box: builds run on your hardware, your keys stay there, and there is no bill.

Pick Codemagic if you have no Mac or want build capacity you don't maintain. Pick XPath if you already release from a Mac and want the release, its keys and its approvals on that machine.

Who should pick which

Who should pick which

Pick Codemagic if

  • you have no Mac, or the Macs you have are not free to build;
  • you want many builds in parallel without buying hardware;
  • you don't want to maintain Xcode, Flutter versions or a machine that has to stay online;
  • a team shares one account with members, roles and SSO;
  • you ship apps in React Native, Unity or native code too.

Pick XPath if

  • you already release Flutter apps from a Mac and want that Mac to do it the same way every time;
  • your keystores and store keys should not leave your machine;
  • you want an AI agent to start builds and releases but never approve one;
  • you want the store review and the release branch followed through to the merge;
  • you would rather not pay per build minute.

FAQ

Questions about XPath and Codemagic

Is XPath a replacement for Codemagic?

For releasing Flutter apps from a Mac you own, yes: XPath builds, signs, uploads to App Store Connect, Google Play and Firebase App Distribution, runs Shorebird releases and patches, and tracks store review. It does not replace Codemagic's hosted machines, its support for other frameworks, or its team accounts.

Can I use XPath without a Mac?

Only for Android. iOS builds need a Mac with Xcode, and App Store uploads run on the XPath engine, which then has to be a Mac. Without any Mac, a hosted service such as Codemagic is the practical choice for iOS.

Which one costs less?

XPath has no price: it is free under Apache-2.0, and you supply the machines and keep them running. Codemagic includes 500 macOS M2 build minutes a month on its free plan, then charges per minute on pay-as-you-go ($0.095 per minute on an M2 Mac mini) or a fixed yearly price from $3,990. Which costs less depends on how many minutes you build and what your own time and hardware are worth.

Where do my signing keys live?

With XPath, on the machine that runs the engine: the macOS Keychain or an encrypted file on Linux. A paired runner receives the keystore only for the job it runs. With Codemagic, you store signing files and keys in Codemagic as secret environment variables, which it encrypts and passes to the build machine while a build runs.

Can I use both?

Yes. XPath's scan notices Codemagic workflow files in a repository and leaves them alone, so you can keep Codemagic for pull-request checks and release from your own Mac with XPath, or the other way round.
